# v0.7 — mobile view

Logged 2026-10-01. Tag: `v0.7`. No plan document; the changes were asked for directly, the
lot's straight after the v0.6 tag and the phone's on the day of this one.

## What changed from v0.6

- **The dev server can be opened from a phone.** `bun run dev --host` listens on every interface
  and prints the address to type on another device on the same network. Without the flag it now
  listens on this machine only.
- **A phone held upright is asked to turn.** The screen dims over a still of where things were,
  a phone turns a quarter under an arrow, and it says *Turn your phone sideways — everything is
  paused until you do.* Behind it no frames are drawn, no engine or weather sound plays, and
  nothing takes a tap. Turned, everything picks up where it stopped.
- **A phone on its side gets the narrow layout.** The badge goes top-left, and the controls sit
  in one row along the bottom, kept clear of the notch. That row now fits an iPhone SE. Opening
  the radio clears both corners so the panel has the screen, and the panel scrolls if the
  screen is shorter than it is. The location picker hangs below the badge and scrolls too.
- **Touch words.** The Park hint says *Tap Chill* rather than *Press F* on a touch screen, and
  on a phone the keyboard shortcuts leave the settings menu.
- **Every vehicle is framed at the camper's width.** `CAMERA.VIEW_SIZE` is 8.0 for all three.
  `RADIO_ZOOM` is rescaled by the same factor, so the radio push-in shows exactly what it did,
  and a test holds that height equal across the specs. At 5.2 the small cars sat in a frame
  the lot was mostly outside of.
- **The park beyond the kerb grew.** The vehicle is still nosed up to the kerb at
  `LOT.KERB_X`. It is the green ahead that runs forward now: twelve metres of grass, seven
  pines and four bushes at every depth, and the lamp on a footing just past the kerb. The
  camper's table stands on the grass in front of it: a white top on black stands, a chair
  either side, the lantern. The cooler is gone. So is the wheel stop, which read as a white
  block under the nose.
- **A moka pot on the camper's hob**, steaming while parked.
- **A full second to fade out across Park** (`INTERACTION.MODE_FADE_MS`). A swap is an exchange
  and this is leaving somewhere. The fade back stays the swap's.

## Notes

**The default was every interface.** Bun.serve with no `hostname` listens on all of them, so the
dev server had been reachable from every network the laptop joined, and `--host` would have
been accepted and ignored. Opting in is the fix, rather than adding a flag that changes nothing.
Opened from another device, the page is plain http on a LAN address, which is not a secure
context. Browsers refuse geolocation there, so the weather falls back to Kuala Lumpur until a
town is picked from the badge.

**Upright is a hidden tab.** There is no portrait layout, deliberately. The view is a wide
isometric shot, and a portrait phone shows a sliver of it with the car small in the middle. So
upright, the app does what it already does when the tab is hidden. The loop stops through
`stop()`, whose `wanted` flag already composes with visibility, so a tab coming back into view
does not restart it behind the prompt. The audio is held with `mixer.hold()`, which replaced
the never-called `suspend()`. A hold has to be sticky, because two things resume the context on
their own: the tab coming back into view, and the first gesture. A gesture made while held
still unlocks the context, since some browsers need a gesture for the first resume. The context
is then suspended straight away. `mixer.test.ts` covers exactly that sequence. The radio stream
and Spotify are not in the audio graph, for the v0.5 reasons, so they carry on, as they would
in a background tab.

**The dim needs a picture under it.** Resizing a canvas clears it, and turning a phone is a
resize, so a stopped loop would leave the prompt over a blank screen. While upright, `main.ts`
draws one still after each resize and once on stopping. That covers both orders the resize and
the media-query change can arrive in.

**What counts as a phone.** A touch screen whose short side is at most 600 px. That is two
strings in `overlay.ts`, `PHONE_UPRIGHT` and `PHONE_SIDEWAYS`, and the CSS and `matchMedia` both
read them, so the layout and the pause cannot disagree about which device they are on. A tablet
is wider than that and keeps the desktop layout. A narrow desktop window has a fine pointer, so
it keeps the narrow layout and never sees the prompt.

**Two old bugs in the narrow layout**, found by putting a phone on its side:
- `wrap-reverse` swaps the cross axis, so `align-items: flex-end` had been aligning the controls
  to the *top* of their line, and the mode buttons hung below the rest.
- The settings menu is right-aligned, which assumes its trigger sits at the right end of the
  row. The narrow layout puts that trigger at the left, so the menu hung off the screen.

Menus now keep themselves on screen whatever the layout (`menu.ts`, on open), rather than each
one guessing where it sits. On a phone the Park hint floats above the mode buttons instead of
standing in the row. Standing there, it widened the row by a third, which on the SE pushed the
row onto two lines. Checked at 667×375, 800×360, 844×390 and 932×430: one row each time, nothing
overlapping the badge, nothing off screen.

**The steam is the exhaust.** The pot's plume is `createExhaust` given a different profile: no
drift, a fraction of the size, a cool grey and a higher alpha, because off-white steam at half
alpha cannot be seen against a cream wall. `createExhaust` takes the plume block now rather than
the whole motion profile, and the body hands the spout back as `steamTip`.

## Unchanged

The vehicles and their bodies, the engine voice and the mix, weather and the lighting director,
Chill and Focus, the radio's three sources, and the desktop layout.

## Deferred

- A portrait layout, by choice rather than for lack of time.
- Fullscreen and an orientation lock. On Android `screen.orientation.lock` needs fullscreen
  first, and on an iPhone a page has neither.
- A home-screen manifest.
- HTTPS for the LAN dev server, so a phone can share its location.
- At Low quality the pixel ratio is 1, which is soft on a 3× screen.
- Keys from a hardware keyboard still reach the registry while a phone is upright.
