# v0.4 — full-height camper

Logged 2026-09-23. Tag: `v0.4`.

## What changed from v0.3

- **Swaps happen behind a curtain.** The screen fades to black, and only then does the camera
  jump, the body change and its shader programs link. The fade back always reveals a vehicle
  that has already been drawn once. This also fixed the warm-up: `renderer.compile` walks the
  scene with `traverseVisible`, so v0.3 was compiling the body on its way out and warming
  nothing at all.
- **The "Volume" label became a mute button.** A speaker icon that toggles, with the slider
  moving to zero and back. Mute is not a second piece of state — muted *is* zero, so the slider
  always shows the level being heard and dragging it to zero is a mute by construction. The
  button only remembers where to come back to.
- **The camper is 2.58 m tall**, which is what a 5.6 m panel van with a high top measures. It
  was 1.95 m, a number solved for rather than observed.
- **Tall bodies leave two walls out.** At 2.58 m a near wall shows you nothing, so the two walls
  this camera looks through — near (−z) and rear (−x) — are not built at all, exactly as the roof
  is not built. The engine bay keeps its whole wing.

  A first pass drew them translucent instead, at 0.58 opacity. It was rejected: half a wall is
  not the same conceit as no roof, and the camper read as a display case.

## Unchanged

The hatchback and the roadster, to the draw call (117 and 106). Camera angles, the treadmill,
weather, lighting, the radio push-in and Spotify panel, the location picker, persistence,
quality tiers, the engine voice and the driver.

## Notes

**Still no cutaways.** The rule from v0.2 holds. What is left out is a *panel*, never a slice
through the body — the same thing the roof has always been. And what stays is the frame, the way
the hatchback keeps its window frames and header rails with no roof between them: the camper
keeps its wall-top rail, two near posts, the rear pillars that now carry its tail lights, the
skirt along the sill, and a wheel arch over each near wheel.

Those arches are not decoration. A wheel centre sits at `y = wheelRadius`, so a wheel reaches
`2r` — 0.72 m against a 0.59 m cabin floor. Delete the side panel without leaving a lip and the
wheel rises into the living room. The front one comes free with the engine bay wing; the rear one
is a crescent built for the purpose.

**What went with the panels.** The near window glass and the rear window (no wall to set them
in), the near curtain, the near decals, and the awning roll — a 3.4 m cylinder on the near plane
is drawn over the open cabin and reads as a bar across the middle of the room. The frame gets
away with that; the awning did not. Rain still reads: with the near wall gone the far windows
are visible through the opening, and the droplets land on those.

**The threshold is derived, not declared.** `floorExposure(dims)` solves the wall equation
`y + z + wallZ > wallTopY` at the floor and returns the fraction of cabin floor on show —
hatchback 0.31, roadster 0.42, camper 0. Below `SEE_THROUGH_BELOW` (0.2), `seeThrough(v)` is
true and the body must build those two walls from `m.ghost`. No spec can claim a roof height
its own interior does not survive.

**Still one material set.** Leaving a panel out adds no material and no draw call — the camper
went from 133 draw calls to 125 — so the v0.3 rule that no vehicle may add a material key never
came under pressure.

**What the height bought.** Overhead lockers in the half-metre the roof gained, a raked front
face so the high top reads as a pod bonded to a van, the window band lifted onto the belt line
above the worktop, and the paper mobile moved onto the front cross-rail from the mid-air it had
been hanging in since v0.3.
