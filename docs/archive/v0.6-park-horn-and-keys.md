# v0.6 — park, horn and keys

Logged 2026-09-28. Tag: `v0.6`. The second half followed [PLAN-park.md](../PLAN-park.md); the
first half shipped against the first three GitHub issues.

## What changed from v0.5

- **The app opens parked.** A third mode, **Park**, in front of the two that existed: an empty
  parking lot on the same plinth, engine off, accessory power on — cabin light and radio. The
  vehicle sits nose-in to a kerb in the middle bay of a row of three, the aisle behind it, a
  verge with one lamp post and a couple of trees ahead. No other cars. **P** parks from
  anywhere; **F** pulls out onto the roadside and starts the engine, and from there is the
  Chill ⇄ Focus toggle it always was. Focus cannot be entered from Park, by key or by click.
  Mode is no longer remembered between sessions; the vehicle still is.
- **The start screen is gone.** Park *is* the still, silent diorama it stood in front of, and any
  first press or click is the gesture Web Audio needed it for. One line of hint above the mode
  control says what to press, until you have.
- **The camper stops for the night.** Parked, it has a picnic table on the verge beside the
  kerb, two folding chairs, a cooler and a lantern that throws a warm pool of its own after
  dark. The near cab seat has been swivelled to face the table since v0.4; now that is true.
- **Parked, the driver's hands come off the wheel** and rest in the lap.
- **Listen says what it is doing.** Opening a stream takes as long as somebody else's server
  takes, and the button used to show nothing for that whole time. It has three states now:
  Listen, then *Tuning…* with a ring turning until you can actually hear something, then Stop.
  Pressing it again mid-tune gives up on the stream, and a station that neither plays nor
  errors is abandoned at `RADIO.CONNECT_TIMEOUT_MS`.
- **One keyboard.** Every key goes through a registry
  ([src/ui/hotkeys.ts](../../src/ui/hotkeys.ts)) that holds the guards once, and the shortcut
  list in the settings menu is that registry read back. **P** park, **F** pull out or toggle,
  **V** vehicle, **M** mute, **H** horn, **R** radio.
- **A horn on every vehicle.** Hold **H**, or press the middle of the steering wheel; holding
  holds it and a click is a tap. Two tones roughly a minor third apart, a sawtooth pair through
  a peak at the flare's mode: 400/500 Hz on the hatchback, 440/550 on the roadster, 310/390 on
  the camper. It works with the engine off, as a horn does.

## Notes

**Park means engine off.** The alternative — Park as Chill in a different place, engine idling
— makes the two modes the same thing with different scenery, and leaves the start screen with a
job. So the engine runs exactly when the vehicle is not parked, which retired `engineOn`: a
derived fact must not be a second field that can disagree with the first. Lighting gained two
circuits to say it — `accessory` for the cabin light and the living light, `ignition` for the
dash and the headlights — and the engine dies faster than it catches (`ENGINE_OFF_OMEGA`),
because a real one stops in half a second and the note must not trail on into the lot.

**The transition is the curtain.** Into or out of Park the world under the vehicle changes,
and that crosses the same fade a vehicle swap uses, sharing its flag so neither can start while
the other has the screen black: the engine cranks in the dark and the fade back reveals the
roadside with it running, or the speed is reset rather than sprung down and the lot comes up
silent. Chill ⇄ Focus is untouched. Driving into the lot — the road sliding away under a
stationary vehicle — is a treadmill with two surfaces and a join, and is deferred, not
rejected. What the tick reads is the mode *on screen*, which lags the store by that curtain the
way `car` lags `store.vehicle`; the store's value is the request.

**One World.** The road and the scenery were two objects that main.ts and the director each
talked to separately; a second place would have made it four and eight. They went behind one
interface first, as a null change — 117 draw calls and 55,876 triangles before and after — and
the lot implements the same. Both stand behind a face that is itself a `World`, so nothing
above knows which is under the wheels. Night and wetness go to both, so the hidden one does not
pop when it comes back; lamp heads come from the one on show.

**The camera rule, as an inequality.** The treadmill has always kept tall things on the far
verge, because from the rear-left quarter anything tall on the near side sweeps between the
lens and the cabin. The lot states the rule: the camera looks along `d ∝ (+1, −k, +1)`, so a
prop can stand in front of a cabin point only if both its x and z are smaller — which means
anything entirely ahead of the nose or entirely beyond the far wall is clear however tall, and
the near-rear quadrant may hold nothing but paint. Every standing thing in the lot is a row in a
table with its box, and a test runs the inequality over the table for every spec in `VEHICLES`.
That test is how the kerb came to be at 4 m rather than the 3.2 the plan proposed: the longest
nose in the registry is 3.5 m from the origin. It is also why the vehicle is nose-in with the
aisle behind it — an empty aisle is exactly nothing tall — and why a camper's table can stand
ahead of the nose without a single part of it over the open cabin.

**No new material, no new light.** The lot is one vertex-colour mesh, one emissive lamp head
and a painted surface. The sodium pool on the nose at night is one of the two streetlight
point lights pointed here instead, and the lantern on the camper's table is the other, at a
lantern's level — `lampHeads` carries a level per head now, which on the road world is always 1.
111 draw calls for the lot against the roadside's 117; 125 with the camper's furniture.

**Where the loading state could have stuck.** An error raised while a station was connecting
used to be ignored, because the handler only ran while `playing`; a stream that failed on the
way up would have left the dial turning for good. And `stalled` shared that handler, which would
have skipped past any station that merely took a moment to fill its first buffer. They are
separate handlers now; while connecting, the timeout is the judge.

**The keyboard registry.** Three controls each brought their own `keydown` listener and their
own copy of the same guards, and the shortcut sentence in the settings menu was a fourth thing
to keep in step by hand. Hoisting the guards moved `preventDefault` on the space bar with them,
which would have stopped space scrolling an open popover on a vehicle with nothing bound to it;
`onDown` may return `false` to decline a press. The list is drawn each time the menu opens.

**The horn, measured.** At the master tap with the volume at its default, a held horn first
came out at 0.92 on the heaviest vehicle — level with the ceiling the camper reaches in Focus
under thunder, which the headroom note in `AUDIO` says nothing new may raise. Equal gain is not
equal loudness an octave down. The level and the per-vehicle trims were set by measurement and
every vehicle lands between 0.56 and 0.67, limiter untouched. The press point costs the bodies
nothing: every spec already says where the wheel is, and a test holds the horn's hitbox clear of
the radio's on every spec.

## Unchanged

The three vehicles and their bodies, the swap fade, the engine voice, weather and the lighting
director, the location picker, quality tiers, Spotify and the tuner's band. Chill and Focus
themselves.

## Deferred

Driving into and out of the lot as a real pull-out on F. The engine ticking as it cools after
P; a distant road under the lot's ambience. Puddles, and the lamp reflected in them. A second
figure at the picnic table. A day/night override, so the lot can be seen at night without
waiting for it. The scene radio still does not turn its dial with the tuner.
